# Developer's Wisdom
Developer's Wisdom is a living collection of principles discovered while building Michael's Foundations. Each principle represents a lesson learned through experience and will guide future software design, architecture, and educational development.

## Principle 1 - The Consistency Principle

> Consistency is more important than decoration. A clean, consistent interface builds trust and makes software easier to use.

---

## Principle 2 - The Clarity Principle

> The user should never have to guess what to do next.

---

## Principle 3 - The Discovery Principle

> Debugging is not failure. It is the process of discovering what the code is actually doing.

---

## Principle #4 - The Organization Principle

> Organize your code so someone can find what they need in seconds, not minutes.

---

## Principle #5 - The Communication Principle

>Professional software is not defined by how little code you write. It is defined by how clearly your code communicates its purpose.

---
## Principle #6 - The Pattern Principle
>When two things look alike, compare them. When they behave alike, combine them.

---
## Principle #7 – The Engagement Principle
> Every interaction should inspire the next one.

- Whether designing software, writing a résumé, building a portfolio, teaching a lesson, or interviewing for a position, the goal is not simply to present information.

- The goal is to create enough value that the other person naturally wants to continue. A résumé should make someone want to schedule an interview. A portfolio should make someone want to explore another project. A learning platform should make someone eager to begin the next lesson.

- An interview should leave the hiring team wishing they had more time to continue the conversation. Design every experience so the next step feels like the obvious choice.

---
## Principle #8 – The First Impression Principle
> Why should I stay?
- Does it earn their time and effort?

## Principle #9 - The Learner Principle
> Meet learners where they are, then guide them to where they want to be.

---

## Principle #10 - Build to Discover
> The first version is not built to be perfect. It is built to teach us what the second version should become.

---

## Principle #11 - Intentional Development
>Build with pupose. Refactor with confidence

---

## Principle #12 - Find the Owner
>which component owns this behavior?

## Principle #13 – One Page, One Purpose

>Every page should answer one primary question for the user.
For example:

- Homepage: Why should I begin?
- Learning Dashboard: What should I do next?
- Lesson Page: What should I learn today?
- Progress Page: How far have I come?
