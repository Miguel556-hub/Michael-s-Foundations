About Me

My path to software development has taken me through several very different chapters. I served in the United States Marine Corps for four years as an Aircraft Mechanic, working on the A4, then the F18. I then served in the Army for an additional 17 years as a Linguist, using my Spanish and Russian language skills in two very different parts of the world with very different missions. My military career strengthened my interest in leadership, language, culture, learning, discipline, and problem-solving.

After retiring from military service, my career took me into the corporate world, where I worked for defense contractors Boeing and Lockheed Martin for a combined nine years. At Boeing, I was assigned to the SBX-1 project for the Missile Defense Agency, serving as the Security Manager for this classified project. I later moved to Lockheed Martin as the Security Manager for the Distributed Mission Operations Center at Kirtland Air Force Base. Although my title was the same, the two positions were not. Each required me to understand a new technical environment, work with new people, take on new responsibilities, and apply what I knew in a new setting.

I later switched gears to become a professional educator, working initially as a skilled trainer at the TJX Corporation. I then transitioned to Teaching, since I was already a certified Teacher. I taught Computer Science at multiple levels, along with Spanish, Russian, and Finance Management. Teaching became more than the subjects I taught. I wanted students to develop skills that would serve them well beyond high school and discover that something difficult could eventually become something they could do. I continue to follow that same philosophy through Spartan Races and Arm Wrestling. More recently, I took on another challenge: Software Development. That journey led me to this project: Michael's Foundations.

Michael's Foundations gives me a place to bring together and share what I have learned along the way. My intent is to keep building, experimenting, exploring new technologies, and finding better ways to work. It also connects with an idea that grew from my Lean Six Sigma background: the Productivity Architect. Here I can create interactive projects around subjects that have been part of my own journey while continuing to learn something new myself. I am still learning, still building, and still asking the same question I required of my students: How do I reach for and achieve what I once thought was impossible?

The Learning Experience I Built

Why I Built This

Michael's Foundations grew from a simple idea: use technology to create learning experiences that help people understand something new, practice it, and build confidence along the way. I wanted the projects I build to reflect the way I taught—clear, encouraging, practical, and centered on the learner.

How I Think About Learning

I believe learning should give people room to try, make mistakes, receive useful feedback, and try again. Getting something wrong is not the end of the learning process; often it is the point where understanding begins. My goal is to create experiences that reduce confusion, encourage curiosity, and help the learner keep moving with confidence.

How I Turned an Idea into Something Real

I started with basic HTML and CSS and began building one piece at a time. As the project grew, I added JavaScript and began turning static pages into interactive learning activities with audio, speech tools, and immediate feedback. I also began creating reusable components and a clearer structure for Michael's Foundations. Many ideas evolved as I built them. Some were moved, simplified, rebuilt, or removed when an improved solution became clear.

What I Learned Along the Way

Building Michael's Foundations has reminded me that software development is a learning process of its own. I have learned to slow down when something breaks, understand the problem before trying to "just fix it," and recognize when rebuilding from a clean foundation is the better solution. I have also learned to make decisions by thinking about what a potential user would see, feel, and experience while exploring Michael's Foundations.
